Good exercise technique means moving through a controlled range of motion with a stable body position so the target muscles do the work and stress on the joints stays reasonable. Learning technique with light weight before adding load is standard coaching practice.

The back squat is a compound exercise for the thighs and hips. The bar rests on the upper back, the feet are about shoulder width apart, and the lifter keeps the chest up, the trunk braced, and the knees tracking in line with the toes while lowering to a comfortable depth and then standing up.

The bench press trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps. The lifter lies on the bench with the shoulder blades pulled back, the feet planted on the floor, and the bar lowered under control to the lower chest before pressing back up. Wrists should stay stacked over the elbows.

The deadlift trains the hips, back, and legs. The bar starts over the middle of the foot, the lifter grips it, braces the trunk, keeps the back neutral, and stands up by pushing the floor away while keeping the bar close to the body. The bar is lowered under control by hinging at the hips.

The barbell row trains the upper back and arms. The lifter hinges forward at the hips with a flat back, pulls the bar toward the lower ribs by driving the elbows back, and lowers it under control without swinging the torso.

The overhead press trains the shoulders and triceps. The lifter braces the trunk and squeezes the glutes to avoid leaning back, presses the bar straight up past the face, and finishes with the arms extended overhead.
