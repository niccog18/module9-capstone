Training volume is the amount of work performed, most simply counted as the number of hard sets per muscle group per week. Total volume can also be described as sets multiplied by repetitions multiplied by weight. Counting weekly sets per muscle group is a practical way to plan and compare programs.

A meta-analysis of weekly resistance training volume found a dose-response relationship with muscle growth. On average, performing about 10 or more sets per muscle group per week produced greater muscle growth than performing fewer than 5 sets per week. More volume is not always better, because the benefit levels off and recovery capacity is limited.

Beginners generally need less volume to progress. A small number of hard sets per muscle group per week can produce good results, and volume can be increased gradually as the lifter adapts.

Training frequency is how often a muscle group is trained per week. A meta-analysis of hypertrophy research found that training a muscle at least twice per week was better for muscle growth than training it once per week. Another meta-analysis found that, when weekly volume was equal, strength gains did not differ clearly between higher and lower frequencies.

In practice, frequency is a way of distributing weekly volume. Splitting 12 weekly sets for a muscle across two or three sessions often allows higher quality work in each session than doing all 12 sets in one day.
