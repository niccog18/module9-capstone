Repetition ranges and training load influence the type of adaptation a workout emphasizes. Lower repetitions with heavier loads tend to favor maximal strength, moderate repetitions favor muscle size and strength, and higher repetitions with lighter loads develop muscular endurance. These ranges overlap, and every range builds some of each quality.

For maximal strength, training guidelines commonly recommend heavier loads for roughly 1 to 6 repetitions per set, with longer rest between sets. Heavy training improves the ability of the nervous system to produce force as well as increasing muscle size.

For muscle growth, a range of about 6 to 12 repetitions per set is widely used, and the American College of Sports Medicine guidance for hypertrophy emphasizes moderate loads in the 8 to 12 repetition range. Research comparing low-load and high-load training found that muscle growth can be similar across a wide range of loads when sets are taken close to muscular failure. Heavier loads produced larger strength gains.

For muscular endurance, lighter loads for about 15 or more repetitions per set are typically used with shorter rest periods. Endurance work trains the muscle to sustain repeated efforts.

Choosing a rep range depends on the goal and the exercise. Heavy compound lifts such as squats and deadlifts are often done in lower rep ranges, while isolation exercises such as curls and lateral raises are often done in higher rep ranges. Mixing rep ranges across a program is common and reasonable.
