RPE stands for rating of perceived exertion. In resistance training it is a number from 1 to 10 describing how hard a set felt, where 10 means maximal effort with no further repetitions possible. RPE lets a lifter adjust the load to how they feel on a given day instead of following a fixed percentage.

RIR stands for repetitions in reserve. It is the number of additional repetitions a lifter believes they could have completed before failing. The two scales describe the same idea: an RPE of 10 is 0 reps in reserve, RPE 9 is about 1 rep in reserve, RPE 8 is about 2 reps in reserve, and RPE 7 is about 3 reps in reserve.

A repetitions-in-reserve based RPE scale was published by Zourdos and colleagues in 2016 for resistance training. Lifters learn the scale by comparing their estimated reps in reserve with how the bar speed slows near the end of a hard set.

Estimating reps in reserve takes practice. Beginners often underestimate how many repetitions they have left, and estimates tend to become more accurate with experience and when the set is closer to failure. Ending sets with 1 to 3 reps in reserve is a common way to train hard while limiting excess fatigue.

In a training plan, effort can be written next to sets and reps, for example 3 sets of 6 to 8 repetitions at RPE 7, which means choosing a weight that leaves about 3 repetitions in reserve.

Reference: Zourdos, M. C., Klemp, A., Dolan, C., et al. (2016). Novel resistance training-specific rating of perceived exertion scale measuring repetitions in reserve. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 30(1), 267-275.
