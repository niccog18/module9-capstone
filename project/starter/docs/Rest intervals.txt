Rest intervals are the time between sets. The length of rest affects how much strength is recovered for the next set, so it changes how much weight can be lifted and how many repetitions can be completed.

For heavy strength work and large compound lifts, rest periods of about 2 to 5 minutes are commonly recommended so that the lifter can maintain performance across sets. The American College of Sports Medicine suggests longer rest, about 2 to 3 minutes, for the heavy core exercises in a program.

A study of resistance-trained men compared 1-minute rest with 3-minute rest between sets. The group resting 3 minutes gained more strength and showed greater muscle growth in some measures, which suggests that very short rest can limit training quality when the goal is strength and size.

For muscle growth, moderate rest of about 1 to 3 minutes is typical, with the longer end used for demanding compound exercises and the shorter end for lighter isolation work. For muscular endurance, shorter rest of about 30 to 60 seconds is common.

Rest can be flexible. A practical rule is to rest until the next set can be performed with good technique and the planned repetitions. Rest times can also be written into a plan in seconds so a prescription such as 3 sets of 6 to 8 repetitions with 180 seconds of rest is unambiguous.
