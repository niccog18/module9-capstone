The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, second edition, recommend that adults get at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity per week, or an equivalent combination of the two.

The guidelines also recommend muscle-strengthening activities of moderate or greater intensity that involve all major muscle groups on two or more days per week. Resistance training with weights, resistance bands, or body weight can meet this recommendation.

Additional health benefits come from doing more than the minimum, up to the upper end of the recommended ranges, and the guidelines note that some physical activity is better than none. People who are new to exercise can start with small amounts and increase gradually.

Cardio exercise such as running, cycling, and swimming can be logged by duration and distance. In a workout log a continuous activity can be recorded as a single set of one repetition together with its total time and distance.
