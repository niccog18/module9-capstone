A repetition, or rep, is one complete execution of an exercise movement, such as lowering and lifting the bar once. A set is a group of consecutive repetitions performed without resting. A workout is made up of exercises, and each exercise is usually performed for several sets.

One-repetition maximum, written 1RM, is the heaviest weight a person can lift for a single repetition with good technique. Loads are often described as a percentage of 1RM, for example 80 percent of 1RM, and 1RM can also be estimated from a harder set of several repetitions.

Muscular failure is the point at which another repetition cannot be completed with good technique. Training to failure is not required for every set, and many programs stop sets one to three repetitions short of failure.

Tempo describes the speed of each phase of a repetition, usually written as numbers for the lowering phase, the pause at the bottom, the lifting phase, and the pause at the top. A tempo of 3-1-1-0 means 3 seconds lowering, a 1 second pause, 1 second lifting, and no pause at the top.

A superset is two exercises performed back to back with little rest between them. Supersets save time, and they can pair opposing muscle groups such as a press followed by a row.

Compound exercises use several joints and muscle groups at once, while isolation exercises focus on one joint and muscle group. Hypertrophy is the increase in muscle size. A training volume number counts the sets, repetitions, or total weight lifted over a period.

A training prescription in this application is a structured set of exercise parameters such as sets, repetition range, load, effort, rest, and tempo. It describes how an exercise is intended to be performed in a plan and is training programming, not medical advice.
